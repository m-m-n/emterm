# Task Plan: task0001 — Chain-aware hold and closing in the write filter

## Goal

The write filter holds a chain of aborted strings and superseded ESCs from
its head until the chain settles or a cut closes it. A cut, or the reader's
fallback closing, drops the whole chain. A later read can then never
complete a construct the live client already closed (finding
3e2024dce619ed9f).

## Design

Follow IMPLEMENTATION.md D1 (chain definition and hold rule), D2 (step 1
and the chain-state part of step 3) and the Shared Components contracts for
the boundary scan (results a, b, d), the write filter feed, the carried-over
completion and the held run as the suppressed tail.

### Boundary scan

- The existing single pass also tracks the current chain head.
  - A construct that opens from ground starts a new chain.
  - The head is kept while each construct is closed by the ESC that opens
    the next string or a lone ESC (an aborted string, or a superseded ESC
    of `ESC ESC`).
  - Any other outcome ends the chain: a completion, a plain byte in ground,
    a complete non-string escape, or an `ESC (` / `ESC )` designation.
- The scan reports the chain head as the hold boundary, and the opening ESC
  of the final incomplete construct as the construct start.
- On a carried run, the scan resumes at the stored construct start, not at
  the chain head. The carried-completion candidate is the construct there.
  The chain prefix before it is never rescanned. This keeps byte-at-a-time
  feeding linear (NFR3, NFR5).

### Feed

- **Normal drain.** Emit through the strip only the bytes before the chain
  head. Keep the chain in `pending`, and store the construct start with
  it.
- **At a cut** (including an empty fed range with a cut, which is how the
  reader's fallback closes the filter). Emit the settled bytes before the
  chain head through the strip, drop everything from the chain head on, and
  reset the chain state. The closing write after those bytes is task0002's
  and task0004's (D2 step 2). This task neither writes nor blocks it.
- **Completion.** When the held construct completes before any cut, report
  the carried-over completion. Its sequence bytes run from the construct
  start through the terminator, and its fed end keeps today's meaning.
  Settle and emit the whole held run.
- **Overflow.** A held chain counts toward the 512 KiB cap. The flush still
  strips the run. Afterwards `pending` is empty, no chain head or construct
  start is stored, and the awaiting flag follows the existing
  end-of-flushed-run rule.
- Update the `feed_with_cuts` and `pending()` docs to the restated FR1
  postcondition: after every call, `pending` is empty or holds exactly the
  chain that ends in the single incomplete construct at the end of the fed
  stream after its last cut.

### Suppressed path

The re-delivered tail is the whole held chain. Excluded pieces are derived
from its length. Confirm with tests that no byte is both an item and part
of the tail. Change the derivation only if a test shows a gap.

## Acceptance Criteria (MANDATORY)

- [ ] AC-1: (FR1, TM-1, registry) The test at the registry path exists and fails on the pre-fix code. It drives the production visibility restore, with the ring not wrapped, through each of the three forms, each followed by every 47 / 1047 / 1049 `h`/`l` pair, and then a later read:
  - `ESC]11;? ESC]0;x`, later read BEL;
  - `ESC]11;? ESC ESC`, later read `\`;
  - `X ESC ESC`, later read `]11;? BEL`.

  It runs each form with the bytes before the switch in one read, and again with the chain head in an earlier read. In every case: no response occurs; the ring holds no OSC introducer from the chain; and the client equals the raw-stream reference in responses, screen, cursor and displayed characters.
- [ ] AC-2: (FR1, TM-1, EC-1, EC-3) Filter level, over a chain corpus of `ESC]11;? ESC]0;x`, `ESC]11;? ESC ESC`, `X ESC ESC`, a DCS aborted by `ESC ]` and an APC aborted by a lone ESC. A cut at any position at or after the chain head:
  - emits no byte from the chain head on;
  - emits the settled bytes before the head through the strip;
  - leaves nothing held.

  The same holds when the head was carried in `pending` from an earlier call, and when an empty fed range with a cut at 0 closes the chain. A later ST therefore cannot complete a dropped DCS.
- [ ] AC-3: (FR1, SPEC AC-4) For every split position and for byte-at-a-time feeding of the chain corpus, with and without a final completion, two things equal the single-call result: the total emitted bytes and `pending`. After every call, `pending` is empty or exactly the chain that ends in the single incomplete construct at the end of the fed stream after its last cut. The chain head is never emitted by the read that opened it.
- [ ] AC-4: (FR1, EC-2, TS-3) When the held construct completes in a later call before any cut:
  - the carried-over completion carries exactly that construct's bytes (from its opening ESC through its terminator) and its fed end;
  - the whole held run is emitted;
  - no aborted chain link is ever reported.

  A chain that settles before a cut is written, and the construct opened after it is dropped by the cut. For a suppressed chunk whose `pending` holds a chain, no byte is both a replacement item and part of the re-delivered tail.
- [ ] AC-5: (FR1, NFR5, TM-2, EC-6, TS-4) A run of aborted strings that grows the held chain past the 512 KiB cap takes the strip-filtered overflow flush and does not panic. Afterwards: `pending` is empty; no chain head or construct start is stored; and the awaiting-designator flag equals the client-parity state at the end of the flushed run. A 64 KiB hostile aborted-introducer stream, fed whole, split and byte at a time, finishes within the test time budget.
- [ ] AC-6: (FR6, SPEC AC-7) The four FR1 tests change exactly as IMPLEMENTATION.md's changed-expectation registry lists, and every other existing test passes unchanged.
  - The round1 record (`test-docs/mux-suppressed-output-fixes/task0002.tests.yaml`) names the new tests and carries the supersede notes, with `red_reason` unchanged. Each updated name resolves in the libtest listing.
  - DECISIONS.md holds the FR1 row (verdict, rationale, the registry test path) and the FR1 behavior-changing tests subsection.
- [ ] AC-7: (NFR1, NFR6) `CARGO_TARGET_DIR=src-tauri/target cargo test --manifest-path src-tauri/Cargo.toml --lib` and `CARGO_TARGET_DIR=src-tauri/target cargo check --manifest-path src-tauri/Cargo.toml --no-default-features` pass.
